Why Do Foreign Cars And EVs Cost More?

It comes down to how much work it takes to get inside your panels without leaving a trace.

  • American cars generally have straightforward panel clips and generous room to route wiring, so the basic package covers them.
  • Foreign cars (Mercedes, BMW, Audi, Lexus, and friends) use more complex trim assemblies and tighter tolerances. More disassembly time, more care, slightly higher price.
  • Electric cars are the most involved. High-voltage systems mean stricter routing rules, and cars like Teslas have minimalist interiors where every wire has to be completely invisible. That's why EVs start at $550.

What Actually Drives The Price

1. Coverage

A basic package lights the main touchpoints — doors, dash, console, footwells. Want the rear doors, seatbacks, trunk, or exterior accents like grille lighting or underglow? Coverage is the biggest variable in your final quote.

2. Fitment quality

There's a real difference between a kit stuck on with adhesive tape and light that looks factory-installed. We tuck every diffuser behind the trim line, hide every wire, and tap power cleanly — no draining your battery, no error codes, no rattles.

3. Control system

Our kits are app-controlled with color-per-zone, brightness, and music-reactive modes. Cheap remotes with 8 colors cost less for a reason.

DIY Kits vs. Professional Install

A $60 Amazon kit and a Saturday afternoon can technically put lights in your car. What it usually also puts in your car: visible wires, sagging light strips after the first summer, blown fuses from cigarette-lighter power taps, and trim panels that never quite click back together. A professional install costs more up front and looks factory for years.

Rule of thumb: if you can see any wiring at all when the door is open, it wasn't installed — it was attached.
